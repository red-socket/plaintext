<Step n={1} title="A shift for every letter">

Caesar uses one shift for the whole message. **Vigenère** uses a keyword to give each letter its own: A means shift 0, B shift 1, up to Z for 25. Write the keyword over the message, repeating it, and shift each letter by the key letter above it.

With the key LEMON, `ATTACK AT DAWN` becomes `LXFOPV EF RNHR`. The two As in ATTACK come out as L and O, because different key letters sat over them.

<Step n={2} title="The table">

Before computers, people used a **tabula recta**: 26 rows, each the alphabet shifted one more place. Find the key letter's row and the plaintext letter's column; the ciphertext is where they meet. The workbench does the same arithmetic.

The cipher is named after Blaise de Vigenère, but Giovan Battista Bellaso described it first, in 1553. For three hundred years it was called *le chiffre indéchiffrable*, the unbreakable cipher.

<Step n={3} title="Breaking it">

It was broken in the 1800s, independently by Charles Babbage and Friedrich Kasiski, with an idea you can do by hand:

1. **Find repeats.** When the same plaintext falls under the same part of the key, it encrypts the same way. Look for repeated chunks of three or more letters.
2. **Measure the gaps.** The distances between repeats are usually multiples of the key length. A length that divides most of them is probably right.
3. **Split into columns.** With a key of length 5, letters 1, 6, 11… were all shifted by the same key letter. Each column is a Caesar cipher, and frequency analysis breaks each one.

The workbench's "Help me find the key" does each step and shows its working.

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="How to spot it">

- Letters only, with normal word shapes if spaces are kept, but **no single shift works**.
- **Letter counts are flat**: no letter stands out the way E does in English.
- The same word encrypts differently in different places.
- The puzzle mentions or hides a **keyword**.

Try any keyword the puzzle offers first. Breaking an unknown key needs a long text: a few hundred letters at least.

</Step>
