<Step n={1} title="Sound as a picture">

A **spectrogram** draws a sound as a picture: time runs left to right, pitch runs bottom to top, and brightness shows how loud each pitch is. A whistle is a bright line; speech is a set of shifting bands; a drum hit is a vertical smear.

</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Drawing with sound">

If a spectrogram turns sound into a picture, you can go the other way: choose a picture, and play a tone for every bright pixel. Heard, it's hiss and warble. Seen, it's a face or a word.

Aphex Twin hid a face in a track on the *Windowlicker* single in 1999, and Nine Inch Nails hid images in songs as part of the *Year Zero* ARG in 2007. Puzzle hunts have done it ever since.

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Too high to hear">

Young adults hear up to about **20 kHz**, and the limit drops with age. A message at 17 or 18 kHz is silent to most people over thirty, but clear on a spectrogram. When you look, raise the top frequency all the way.

<QuickCheck id="spectro-limit" answer={["20", "TWENTY", "20KHZ"]} note="About 20 kHz, and less as you get older.">Roughly the highest pitch young adults can hear, in kHz?</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="How to spot it">

- **Noise, chirps or warbling** with no rhythm or tune.
- A sound file where you'd expect an image, or a puzzle that says **look**, not listen.
- Silence that's suspiciously long: something may be above your hearing.

</Step>
