<Step n={1} title="Draw the cell">

Draw two tic-tac-toe grids and two X shapes. Fill the first grid with A to I, the second with J to R, the first X with S to V, and the second X with W to Z. The letters in the second grid and second X each get a **dot**.

Now each letter is written as the **shape of its cell**: A, in the top-left corner, is just a right side and a bottom (`⌟`). E, in the middle, is a full square. S, at the top of the first X, is a V.

Freemasons used it in the 1700s, which is why it's sometimes called the Masonic cipher.

<Step n={2} title="Other keys">

Pigpen has variants: some put the letters in the X shapes in a different order, some fill the grids differently, some put the dots elsewhere. If most letters come out right and a few look wrong, the puzzle probably uses another key. Work out the odd ones from the words around them.

<QuickCheck id="pigpen-n" answer="N" note="The middle cell of the second grid: a full square with a dot.">Which letter is a full square with a dot inside?</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="How to spot it">

- **Symbols made of right angles and V shapes**, some with dots.
- The same shape turns up **with and without a dot**.
- About 26 different symbols, in word-length groups.

Lookalikes include the Templar cipher (based on a cross) and Rosicrucian variants. The method is the same: work out the grid, then read the cells.

</Step>
