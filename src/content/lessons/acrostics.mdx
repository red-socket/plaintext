<Step n={1} title="Read down, not across">

An **acrostic** hides a word in the first letters of each line. Poets have done it for thousands of years; Lewis Carroll ended *Through the Looking-Glass* with a poem whose first letters spell the full name of the real Alice.

> Cold the wind across the moor,\
> Over hill and under door;\
> Darkness gathers, rain will fall,\
> Every window shuttered all.

</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Null ciphers">

A **null cipher** hides a message in an innocent text by a rule: the first letter of every word, the second letter, every fifth word. Spies liked them because ciphertext looks suspicious and a chatty letter home doesn't.

<QuickCheck id="acrostic-seven" answer="SEVEN" note="Send, every, vessel, east, now.">Read the first letter of each word: *Send every vessel east now.*</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Every rule you can think of">

When a text feels slightly forced, try the cheap rules one after another: first letters of lines, of words, last letters, second letters, every second or third letter, only the capitals. The reader below does each one instantly.

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="How to spot it">

- A poem or letter that's **slightly stilted**, as if words were picked for their letters.
- **Odd capitals**, or lines that start oddly.
- A puzzle that tells you to read **carefully** or **between the lines**.

</Step>
