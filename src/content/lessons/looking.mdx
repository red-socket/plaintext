<Step n={1} title="Encoding, cipher or hash">

Hidden messages come in three kinds, and knowing which one you have tells you what's possible.

- An **encoding** is a public rule. Anyone with the chart can read it: Morse code, binary, Base64. There is no secret, just a different way of writing.
- A **cipher** needs a key. The method can be known (shift every letter), but without the key (by how much?) you have to work it out.
- A **hash** is a one-way fingerprint. You can't turn it back into the original; you can only guess the original, hash your guess and compare.

Puzzles use all three, but encodings are the most common, because they're fair: everything you need is on the page.

<Step n={2} title="Look before you decode">

Most of solving is noticing. Before reaching for a tool, ask:

- **Which characters appear?** Only letters? Only digits? Two symbols? Letters, digits and `=`?
- **How are they grouped?** Pairs, fives, eights, words of normal length?
- **What repeats?** A repeated chunk is a foothold.
- **What's outside the text?** The title, a picture, a date, a number in the corner. Puzzle makers rarely waste words.

The [field guide](/field-guide) is organised by exactly these observations. Start from what you see, not from a cipher's name.

<QuickCheck id="looking-polybius-digits" answer={["5", "FIVE"]} note="Pairs of digits from 1 to 5 point to a Polybius square, a 5×5 grid of letters.">Look at `23 15 31 31 34`. Every digit is between 1 and which number?</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Try the cheap things first">

Some attempts take seconds, others take an hour. Do the fast ones first.

1. **Seconds:** read it as A1Z26, try ROT13, decode it as Base64, read it backwards.
2. **Minutes:** try every Caesar shift, count letter frequencies, look for a keyword in the puzzle.
3. **Longer:** everything else.

The title and the story often tell you which one to try. A puzzle called *Dot dot dash* is probably Morse; a letter "dated the Ides of March" probably has a key of 15.

<QuickCheck id="looking-sixty-four" answer={["BASE64", "BASE 64"]} note="The title says 64, and = at the end is how Base64 pads its output.">A puzzle is called *Sixty-four*, and its text ends with `==`. What do you try first?</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="When you're stuck">

- **Write down what you tried.** It stops you going in circles.
- **Re-read the prompt** and look for the one detail you ignored. Fair puzzles contain everything you need.
- **Check for another layer.** If your decoded text still looks encoded, it probably is. Decode it again.
- **Use a hint.** They start small. Taking one isn't failing; staying stuck for an hour isn't learning.

</Step>
