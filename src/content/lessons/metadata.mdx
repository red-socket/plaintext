<Step n={1} title="Files that describe themselves">

A photo from a camera or phone usually carries **EXIF** data inside the file: the camera model, the date and time, the exposure settings, sometimes a description or comment, and often the **GPS position**. None of it shows in the picture.

<Step n={2} title="Where it was taken">

Phones store latitude and longitude with almost every photo. Latitude says how far north or south; longitude, how far east or west. Put the two into a map and you're standing where the photographer stood.

It's also why many sites strip metadata from uploads. Puzzle files you download directly usually keep theirs.

<QuickCheck id="metadata-latitude" answer={["NORTH SOUTH", "NORTH AND SOUTH", "NS"]} note="Latitude is north–south, longitude is east–west.">Does latitude measure north–south or east–west?</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Other files, other notes">

Every format has its own place for notes:

- **PNG** images have text chunks, named `tEXt`, `zTXt` and `iTXt`, which can hold anything.
- **PDF** and **Word** files record an author, a title and the software that made them.
- **MP3** files have ID3 tags: artist, album, comments, even pictures.
- Even the **file name** can be a clue.

<QuickCheck id="metadata-png" answer="PNG" note="tEXt chunks are part of the PNG format.">Which image format stores notes in chunks called tEXt?</QuickCheck>

</Step>

<Step n={4} title="How to spot it">

- A puzzle that gives you **a file**, not just a picture.
- A question about **where, when or who** that the picture itself can't answer.

Open every file in a metadata viewer before doing anything clever. It takes seconds.

</Step>
